1. STRATA
================================================================================

TITLE
strata — a memory rebuilt, in three modes | 15 min dark ambient

DESCRIPTION
Dark ambient ritual music: a 15-minute piece in D minor at 76 BPM for kalimba, timpani, frame drums, bowed strings and drone, where one three-note refrain is buried and recovered over three movements.

The refrain is the memory. It opens as three notes on a kalimba (root, sixth, octave), is lost, and is rebuilt a note at a time until the whole line plays in every voice at once, and then alone. The notes on the page never change: the two degrees the modes move under them do the work, so the same line reads grave in D aeolian (wood), a semitone darker in D phrygian (stone) and warm in D dorian (air).

Three strata, one ground. 294 bars, 24 sections, written as text and rendered in the browser.

TITLE
arrival — an orchestral first contact, and nobody fires a shot

DESCRIPTION
Cinematic orchestral score: first contact in D minor at 64 BPM, for piano, harp, recorders, handchimes, tubular bells, organ pedal, bowed strings and timpani. Something is coming, and nobody fires a shot.

Two wills, side by side. The human theme (piano, harp, recorders) rises and follows the harmony. The alien signal (handchimes, tubular bells) never moves with the chords and never changes its intervals, so it stays foreign no matter what happens underneath it. Fear lives in the low half, hope in the high half, and the two climaxes play both at once over the same chords instead of letting either win.

================================================================================

TITLE
ascent — three notes from a cave to orbit, six eras, one pulse

DESCRIPTION
Six eras of music from one motif: the same three notes carried from a stone cave to orbit at a steady 108 BPM, as the instruments, harmony, room and density change around a pulse that never does.

The motif is 0 2 4. It grows a note or a leap per era, picks up a second phrase in the electric age, and in orbit the machine plays it upside down over an ocarina still whispering it the right way up. The climax is uneasy on purpose: phrygian, the flat second grinding against the tonic, a counting voice ticking off the bars. The rooms move with the eras, hall to plate to the small box of a capsule.

TITLE
black-ice — dark 90s club techno for a 1995 hacker movie, 132 BPM

DESCRIPTION
Mid-90s club techno: dark, hypnotic and mechanical, 132 BPM in A minor, written for the soundtrack of a 1995 cyberpunk hacker movie that never existed. A 909 groove, a rolling analog bass, a resonant five-note sequence, and the air and voices of Nine Inch Nails recordings.

Six ideas, and nothing new after the first minute. The kick, the bass ostinato, the motif, a high glass sequence, the air and the voice are arranged rather than added to: filters open and close over whole sections, parts drop out and return, the drums thin and come back. The motif never follows the one chord change (A minor to F to G), so it stays a machine while the bass moves under it. The air is sampled: held strings and a drone from Demon Seed, a dub echo and three wordless screams from Ghosts 38, each fading in under the parts rather than over them.

WHAT ELSE TO FILL IN
================================================================================

Links clip, and that is YouTube, not you. YouTube shows only about the first 41 characters of a link and appends "...". The link still works when clicked; only the visible text is cut. That is why every link above has a line before it saying where it goes, so the cut text costs the reader nothing. The site address is also written out as plain words in the first sentence, where it cannot be clipped.

If you want links that display in full, the address itself has to get shorter. "https://jonborchardt.github.io/strudel-bench/" is already 45 characters before the song name. Two ways out: a short custom domain, or a repo named jonborchardt.github.io (a GitHub user site, which you do not have yet, since the blog is a project page) holding one-line redirect pages at /s/<song>. That gives "https://jonborchardt.github.io/s/strata" at 39 characters, which displays whole. Ask and I will write the redirect files.

Do not paste the YouTube channel URL into a description. YouTube swallows it and renders a channel chip, which is why the last line came out as "/ @jonathanborchardt". The channel is already linked under the video, so the line is gone.
Playlist. Put all seven in one, something like "strudel-bench: songs written as text". Playlists are indexed separately and carry viewers from one track to the next.

Thumbnail. A still from the video's own world, with the song name in large type. They read as a set if the type and its position stay identical across all seven.

Pinned comment. One line plus the play-it-yourself link. The description link sits below the fold; a pinned comment does not.

Category and license. Music. Standard YouTube license is the safer default: the samples are not yours to relicense, and the credit line in the description covers them.

black-ice is different on two counts. Its Nine Inch Nails samples are CC BY-NC-SA 3.0: keep the attribution line in the description, and do not monetize that video, since NonCommercial rules out ads on it. And it is not on the site yet: its pack (samples/user/black-ice-cuts) says deploy: false, so the Pages build leaves the song out, which is why its description has no direct link. Set deploy to true in that pack.json (the license allows it, and the attribution file ships with it), then add the link line back:
https://jonborchardt.github.io/strudel-bench/#black-ice.strudel

End screen. Last 10 seconds, pointing at the next song and the playlist. Every one of these ends quietly, which leaves room for it.

Shorts. Each song has one obvious 30-second moment: strata's whole at 12:37, arrival's threshold at 2:33, machine's chorus 3 at 1:44, ascent's orbit at 2:57, duel's fifth exchange at 2:53, neon's second drop at 1:52, black-ice's peak at 2:40. A vertical crop of the generated video with the same title works as a trailer for the full track.

Consistent naming. Lowercase song name, a dash, then the hook. It makes the channel read as one body of work rather than seven unrelated uploads.
