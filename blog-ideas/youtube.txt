8. HELD
================================================================================

TITLE
held — a slow chamber piece with a low end you can feel, 65 BPM

DESCRIPTION
Modern chamber music: a slow piece in D dorian at 65 BPM for cello, violin, flute, piano, contrabass and a string bed, built around a low end deep enough to feel rather than only hear.

The bottom is in three stages, an octave apart each: a sine on the chord root at d2, the felt fundamental, with a pipe organ pedal an octave under it for body; an arco contrabass over that, sustaining; and a pizzicato on the downbeat, dry and short, because what the body reads as bass is a fast envelope at 65 to 100 Hz while everything else down there holds. The sine took three tries, and the lesson was that the octave matters more than the waveform: at d1, 37 Hz, a laptop or a phone cannot move air that low, and neither level nor saturation rescues it. Above the bottom the cello states the tune, the violin takes it an octave up, the flute answers only in the bars the violin leaves open, and one 3.2-second hall holds all of it.

Four sections, mixed by measurement. D dorian for the raised sixth, so the minor comes out warm rather than sad: Dm, G, C, F.

9. CROOKED
================================================================================

TITLE
crooked — jazz in 11/8, a metre jazz doesn't use | Balkan quartet, E phrygian

DESCRIPTION
Jazz in 11/8: a Balkan band playing jazz changes in E phrygian, grouped 3+3+3+2 so three long groups are followed by a short one and every bar is cut off a beat early. Sax, upright bass, piano and a darbuka where the drum kit would be.

Not a jazz quartet in an odd time signature — a different band entirely. 5/4 and 7/8 are Brubeck's, so this is in 11, which almost no jazz record uses, and the instruments are the ones that play it: a darbuka's doum, tek and ka instead of ride and snare, and a sax that mixes three recorded articulations, plain, vibrato and tongued short, picked per note. Everything melodic in the song is one three-note cell — a note, a rest, a leap of a fourth — which fills one group of the 3+3+3+2 exactly, then gets transposed, inverted, filled in and fragmented. The head states it, the sixteen-bar solo develops it, and the bass quotes it back during its own solo, alone with the drum.

The bridge is the only place the song leaves either the key or the metre, and it leaves both at once. C major is E phrygian's own pitch collection with the tonic moved, so not one new note enters — it simply turns the light on. The metre goes to 4/4 but the pulse does not change: 11/8 at 290 and 4/4 at 145 are the same eighth note, so only the grouping moves, which is what makes the 11 feel like home when it comes back. It ends on Em7, at once the third chord of C major and the tonic of E phrygian, and hands the song over without a seam.

Seven sections, 2:30, mixed by measurement. E phrygian for the flat second, which is the Balkan cadence and a jazz colour at the same time: Em7, F, Em7, Dm7.
