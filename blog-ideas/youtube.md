**tags** line for the tags field, and **hashtags**. Append the universal blurb at the bottom of this file to every

## strata

**Title**
`strata — a memory rebuilt, in three modes | 15 min dark ambient`

**Description**

    Dark ambient ritual music: a 15-minute piece in D minor at 76 BPM for kalimba, timpani, frame drums, bowed
    strings and drone, where one three-note refrain is buried and recovered over three movements.

    The refrain is the memory. It opens as three notes on a kalimba (root, sixth, octave), is lost, and is rebuilt a
    note at a time until the whole line plays in every voice at once, and then alone. The notes on the page never
    change: the two degrees the modes move under them do the work, so the same line reads grave in D aeolian (wood),
    a semitone darker in D phrygian (stone) and warm in D dorian (air).

    Three strata, one ground. 294 bars, 24 sections, written as text and rendered in the browser.

## arrival

**Title**
`arrival — an orchestral first contact, and nobody fires a shot`

**Description**

    Cinematic orchestral score: first contact in D minor at 64 BPM, for piano, harp, recorders, handchimes, tubular
    bells, organ pedal, bowed strings and timpani. Something is coming, and nobody fires a shot.

    Two wills, side by side. The human theme (piano, harp, recorders) rises and follows the harmony. The alien signal
    (handchimes, tubular bells) never moves with the chords and never changes its intervals, so it stays foreign no
    matter what happens underneath it. Fear lives in the low half, hope in the high half, and the two climaxes play
    both at once over the same chords instead of letting either win.

    Written as text, rendered in the browser, no DAW.

## ascent

**Title**
`ascent — three notes from a cave to orbit, six eras, one pulse`

**Description**

    Six eras of music from one motif: the same three notes carried from a stone cave to orbit at a steady 108 BPM,
    as the instruments, harmony, room and density change around a pulse that never does.

    The motif is 0 2 4. It grows a note or a leap per era, picks up a second phrase in the electric age, and in orbit
    the machine plays it upside down over an ocarina still whispering it the right way up. The climax is uneasy on
    purpose: phrygian, the flat second grinding against the tonic, a counting voice ticking off the bars. The rooms
    move with the eras, hall to plate to the small box of a capsule.

    Written as text, rendered in the browser.

## duel

**Title**
`duel — two acoustic guitar players trading solos, six exchanges`

**Description**

    Acoustic guitar duel: two steel-string players trading six solos in E dorian at 125 BPM, over acoustic bass,
    cajon and shakers. Every note is written by hand, none generated.

    Two players, not two sounds. One is tight and bright, close-miked and dry, legato, accenting on the beat, sitting
    left. The other is looser, warmer, roomier, punchier in the attack, leaning on the offbeats, sitting right. They
    play together in the intro, verse, break and pickup, then answer each other solo for solo. In three of the six
    exchanges they step on each other, the next player coming in under the last bar while the first refuses to stop.

Paste this under every description. Swap `<song>` for the song's file name (`strata`, `arrival`, `machine`,
`ascent`, `duel`, `neon`).

    ---

    Play this song, and edit it, in your browser:
    https://jonborchardt.github.io/strudel-bench/#<song>.strudel

    The whole song is a few dozen lines of text. Open the link, press play, drag a slider, change a note, hear it
    immediately. Nothing to install.

    The source of this track:
    https://github.com/jonborchardt/strudel-bench/blob/main/songs/<song>.strudel

    strudel-bench is a live-coding harness where a song is a text file written in a high-level language: parts,
    sections, and twelve musical axes (density, brightness, weight, space, articulation and so on) that a phrase like
    "darker and more spacious" can move. Most of the code and the songs were written by coding agents working from a
    written spec, in the spirit of my blog, "always shippable, always improving":
    https://jonborchardt.github.io/blog/

    The video is not a screen recording. It is generated from the song itself: every hit in the music drives the
    animation, and the world it plays in is chosen from the song's own musical features.

    Built on Strudel, the JavaScript port of TidalCycles: https://strudel.cc
    Samples come from the public Strudel packs, each under its own license: https://strudel.cc/learn/samples/

# What else to fill in

- **Playlist.** Put all six in one, something like "strudel-bench: songs written as text". Playlists are indexed and
  keep people watching past one track.
- **Thumbnail.** A still from the video's own world, with the song name in large type. They will read as a set if the
  type and position stay identical across all six.
- **Pinned comment.** One line plus the play-it-yourself link. The description link is below the fold; the pinned
  comment is not.
- **Category and license.** Music. Consider Creative Commons on the video, but note the samples are not yours to
  relicense, so a plain "standard YouTube license" on the video with the credit line in the description is the safer
  default.
- **End screen.** Last 10 seconds, pointing at the next song and the playlist. Every song here ends quietly, which
  leaves room for it.
- **Shorts.** Each song has one obvious 30-second moment: strata's "whole" at 12:37, arrival's threshold at 2:33,
  machine's chorus 3 at 1:44, ascent's orbit at 2:57, duel's fifth exchange at 2:53, neon's second drop at 1:52.
  A vertical crop of the generated video plus the same title works as a trailer for the full track.
- **Consistent naming.** Lowercase song name, an em-dash-free hyphen, then the hook. It makes the channel read as
  one body of work rather than six unrelated uploads.
